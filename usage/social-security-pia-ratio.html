Population Social Security PIA ratio¶

The reported-benefit route implements design v3 C1–C3, with A1’s factor composition, A5.2’s record/proxy distinctions, P2’s annual cross-section convention, and amendments M2, M4, M8 and M9. It approximates a formula reform for benefits already in payment. It does not reconstruct a worker’s earnings history or establish benefit entitlement.

For each annual Person component X (retirement, disability, survivors, dependents), the population lever applies

z = reported_X / (12 * a * s * baseline_COLA(E, t))
factor_X = new_PIA(inferred_AIME, E; t) / baseline_PIA(inferred_AIME, E)

The public formula multiplies the reported base by this factor, the joint COLA factor, the across-the-board factor and the payable factor. Calculated source routes compute their PIA and COLAs directly and bypass these population factors. An initial public-name input is migrated to its _reported twin, including public-name dataset columns in the scoring year; a later explicit public write is a final override. See the benefit-lever ingress documentation.

The forward PIA calculations and inverse use the same service as the supplied-record calculator. bend_points_at selects the original eligibility-year bend points; formula_path_at supplies the combined reform and its recompute_mode. C does not add a second retroactive-recomputation switch. The inverse always uses the fixed unreformed formula and the unreformed reported amount.

Supplied claim age and explicit scenarios¶

social_security_retirement_claim_age_months is a pure annual Person input: age in whole months at first entitlement, so 62 years 3 months is 747. It has no formula, uprating, or positive-amount selection rule. A projected H5 may store this column like any other input, with a value for each scoring year.

gov.contrib.ssa.benefit_levers.pia_ratio.claiming_scenario explicitly selects:

Value

Claiming adjustment source

supplied_claim_age (default)

The supplied age in whole months.

entitlement_dates

Component entitlement year/month and supplied birth date.

age_62

Sensitivity assuming age 62.

age_65

Sensitivity assuming age 65.

fra

Sensitivity assuming cohort full retirement age.

age_70

Sensitivity assuming age 70.

Core represents these named selector parameters as singleton lists. For example, set claiming_scenario to [age_65] and disability_eligibility_proxy to [current_year] in a parameter reform; the adapter reads the one selected name. Bare scalar strings are unsupported by Core’s parameter schema.

All scenarios preserve cohort FRA, early months and delayed-credit rates. Under supplied_claim_age, a missing value, a value before 62, or a value after 70 leaves the affected factor at one and raises _pia_ratio_missing_claim_age. There is no silent change to another scenario. The entitlement_dates scenario uses the entitlement year/month and birth year/month. Supply social_security_birth_month; the optional social_security_birth_day refines SSA age attainment. With a known birth month and no day, day 2 is the explicit within-month convention and _pia_ratio_proxy is true. Zero birth month means unavailable. A complete ss_calculator_birth_date supplies the exact date instead; exact dates preserve attainment on the day before a birthday, including January 1 births.

Where only a birth year is available for an age-month scenario, the cohort calculation uses the documented non-January-1 approximation and raises _pia_ratio_proxy. Exact birth information and an explicitly supplied birth_year take precedence over a year derived from current age. When birth information is unavailable but a worker eligibility year is supplied, E minus 62 can supply the approximate birth cohort for FRA and credit rates; this does not change the supplied eligibility year. Supplied month/day without an observed birth year or complete birth date still raises the proxy flag. A claiming scenario is substantive: in C2, replacing the correct age-62 adjustment of 0.70 with 1 infers AIME \(2,978 instead of \)5,000 and changes the annual gain from \(1,165.248 to \)1,664.64 before rounding, an overstatement of about 43%.

Eligibility years and auxiliary identification¶

Supplied social_security_X_worker_eligibility_year, social_security_X_worker_id links and the underlying worker’s social_security_worker_computation_year precede proxies. A supplied entitlement/onset year supplies the component’s applicable basis where appropriate. A link must identify an existing Person and a supplied computation basis; conflicting record years are unsupported.

For own retirement, a supplied worker eligibility year takes precedence. Without one, exact birth information or an explicitly supplied birth_year identifies the age-62 eligibility year before the current-age proxy; known entitlement dates and a supplied observed claim age can also identify that year. If none of these supplies a basis, each scoring year’s proxy is E = t - age_t + 62. Rebuild it independently for every annual cross-section. A row whose age stays 70 and has no supplied basis has E=2018 in 2026 and E=2027 in 2035; the model does not carry an inferred cohort or claiming history between those rows. Integer age also leaves birthday/within-year ambiguity, so compare adjacent eligibility years when scoring sensitivities.

Disability uses a = 1 and a supplied onset, entitlement or worker computation year when available. Missing onset is explicitly controlled by gov.contrib.ssa.benefit_levers.pia_ratio.disability_eligibility_proxy: pre_reform is the upper-exposure assumption, using a supported benchmark before the active reform; current_year assumes newly entitled in the scoring year. Both are labelled proxies, not observed onset histories.

For survivors and dependents, a recipient age cannot identify the underlying worker’s PIA. A supplied worker link/year alone does not establish the share. The identified auxiliary route also requires:

  • social_security_X_worker_benefit_ratio: the composite a * s, including every applicable claiming adjustment and recipient share/reduction.

  • social_security_X_worker_ratio_identified: an explicit assertion that this is one identified worker PIA, with no unaccounted dual entitlement, binding family cap or earnings-test withholding.

For reported retirement, a record that also has positive reported survivor, dependent or disability amounts remains unmodeled for this lever. The mixed recipient amounts do not identify whether retirement is one full worker PIA, dual entitlement or a partial-year conversion.

The inversion divides by that composite ratio once. Without a supported worker basis and identified ratio, the amount keeps factor one and raises _pia_ratio_unmodeled. No recipient-age worker proxy is enabled by default. For this composite route, the named claiming-adjustment output is one as a decomposition convention: the entire supplied a * s is assigned to the worker-benefit ratio. This does not establish that the underlying worker claimed at FRA or identify a separately. The restricted supplied-ratio survivor household calculator retains its own more detailed record-basis and nonbinding-limit assertions.

When S.770 is active, reported survivors additionally require the same recipient-side ss_calculator_* computation-basis fields as the household route: worker birth year and death year, basis provenance, prior-disability declaration, ordinary non-WINDEX assertion, and the applicable exact dates or year-only/same-year assertions. An authoritative computation year is required for prior disability. The basis must agree with the supplied worker eligibility or computation year. Missing identification leaves factor one and raises _pia_ratio_unmodeled; contradictory supplied information is rejected. For a pre-2026 death before age 62 with no prior disability eligibility, the existing S.770 interpretation switch controls only the 18% first-bend-point increase. The 95% first factor applies with either switch value.

Supported benchmark years start in 1983. A positive record with an earlier benchmark keeps factor one and raises _pia_ratio_pre_1983. The initial PIA rounding rule before 1983 is outside this inverse’s scope. COLAs accumulate from eligibility, including pre-claim COLAs, using the unreformed statutory ledger; they do not use benefit-income uprating or reformed COLAs.

Named annual outputs¶

Replace X below with any of the four component names. Monetary outputs are monthly dollars at the eligibility-year valuation basis, even though the reported source benefit is annual.

Person variable suffix after social_security_X

Meaning

_inferred_aime

Baseline inverse of the observed amount; the lower-endpoint convention for an exact dime-rounded eligibility PIA.

_inferred_aime_lower_bound

Inclusive conditional AIME interval bound.

_inferred_aime_upper_bound

Exclusive conditional AIME interval bound.

_pia_ratio_eligibility_year

Underlying worker eligibility/computation year E.

_pia_ratio_claiming_adjustment

Imputed worker claiming adjustment a; zero means unavailable on a positive retirement record, while one is the auxiliary composite-ratio decomposition convention.

_pia_ratio_own_record_retired_worker

Own-record retired-worker indicator.

_pia_ratio_missing_claim_age

Required claiming information is unusable.

_pia_ratio_pre_1983

Benchmark predates supported initial-PIA rounding.

_pia_ratio_unmodeled

This amount lacks a supported identified inversion.

_pia_ratio_proxy

An eligibility, birth or claiming approximation was used, including every resolved age_62, age_65, fra or age_70 sensitivity even with an exact birth date.

_pia_ratio_measurement_uncertainty

Annual reported measurement is not an exact eligibility PIA.

Zero monetary outputs on records without an inference are placeholders. Inspect the flags before interpreting them as zero earnings. The own-record indicator describes the modeling classification; it is not evidence of insured status or an administrative validation of benefit type. It is independent of whether claiming data are missing or the benchmark predates 1983. For an AIME distribution or percentile calculation, also exclude _pia_ratio_unmodeled, _pia_ratio_missing_claim_age and _pia_ratio_pre_1983 records and inspect the proxy and measurement-uncertainty flags. For identified-only distributions, also exclude _pia_ratio_proxy records; report their sensitivity results separately. The named AIME outputs include supported proxy inferences, so this filter must be applied by the consumer.

On a positive retirement record, claiming adjustment zero is an unavailable diagnostic sentinel until the selected scenario yields a usable adjustment. Missing claiming data and unsupported pre-1983 records can therefore report zero while keeping the benefit factor at one. Do not interpret this sentinel as an actuarial factor, a complete benefit cut, or a default FRA assumption; filter the associated flags. Disability keeps adjustment one, auxiliary composite ratios use one by decomposition convention, and a zero-benefit record keeps the neutral adjustment one.

For exact dime-rounded baseline eligibility PIA z, the interval is [inverse(z), inverse(z + 0.10)); the point estimate is its lower endpoint. C2’s $2,156.80 baseline PIA therefore gives [5000, 5000.3125). When the source is a rounded paid benefit or a survey annual amount, that narrow interval is conditional on the assumed measurement basis. It does not quantify all survey error. gov.contrib.ssa.benefit_levers.pia_ratio.annual_rounding_bound optionally supplies a nonnegative symmetric annual-dollar bound for [max(0, b - bound), b + bound), propagated backward through the baseline divisor, with one dime of initial PIA truncation added at the upper endpoint. The point estimate continues to invert the observed amount; it does not move to the lower endpoint of this wider measurement interval. The default zero adds no annual measurement-resolution assumption; it does not certify exact data.

Core stores monetary input arrays as float32. Before exact arithmetic, the adapter uses each stored scalar’s shortest float32 decimal representation. This avoids expanding binary representation noise into an additional PIA dime; it does not round every annual source to cents or recover precision already lost when the input was stored.

Named AIME point estimates and interval bounds are stored at Core’s float32 resolution. The adapter rounds them toward positive infinity so an ordinary cast cannot move a lower endpoint below the baseline inverse. Represented values can differ from the mathematical endpoint by up to one float32 step; use the shared pure service for exact interval membership or boundary calculations. It keeps rational endpoints internally, and the shared PIAInverse exposes exact Fraction bounds as well as high-precision decimals.

A zero benefit produces factor one and zero monetary outputs without division. Negative reported amounts and nonpositive divisors are unsupported and rejected. Factors cannot be negative. With every PIA reform off, factors are exactly one.

Weighted counts and scoring limits¶

For any Person indicator flag, the authoritative weighted count is

sum(flag_i * person_weight_i)

The associated unmodeled annual amount is sum(flag_i * social_security_X_reported_i * person_weight_i). Apply weights once. Report identified results, proxy sensitivities and unmodeled amounts separately.

Household variables social_security_X_pia_ratio_FLAG_count are also available for FLAG equal to missing_claim_age, pre_1983, unmodeled, proxy or measurement_uncertainty. They sum the corresponding Person indicators within a household. A normal weighted household aggregation gives the weighted count when members use the household weight. If an artifact supplies distinct Person weights within a household, use the Person-indicator formula above.

This approximation assumes the reported annual amount represents a full-year benefit at the stated valuation basis; partial-year entitlement, survey error, dual entitlement, withheld checks and binding caps can defeat unique inversion. No package-specific parameter values or administrative percentile estimates are encoded here. M8 allows progressive price-indexing thresholds from a supplied bend-point fraction or dollar path; C’s named outputs remain available for population diagnostics and drift checks.
