OASDI modeled liabilities and benefits¶

Six outputs report modeled OASDI liabilities, modeled OASDI benefits and their difference, the modeled balance. They add up existing variables and change no other output. The balance compares modeled liabilities with modeled benefits; it is not a trust-fund cash balance.

Definitions¶

employee_social_security_tax + employer_social_security_tax + self_employment_social_security_tax

tob_revenue_oasdi

Each member’s oasdi_payroll_tax_revenue, plus each tax unit’s oasdi_benefit_taxation_revenue and oasdi_niit_revenue, each counted once

oasdi_cost

Household

Members’ public social_security, after any enabled benefit policies

oasdi_modeled_balance

Household

oasdi_income - oasdi_cost

The payroll leaves read their rates and the wage base from parameters: gov.irs.payroll.social_security.rate.employee and .employer apply to wages up to gov.irs.payroll.social_security.cap, and gov.irs.self_employment.rate.social_security applies to self-employment net earnings in the room that wages leave under the base. A reform that replaces a leaf reaches the sum through that leaf. The owners’ draw reform’s self_employment_social_security_tax adds owners_draw_oasdi_tax, so the draw’s OASDI tax counts once; don’t add it again.

oasdi_benefit_taxation_revenue is the OASDI share of income tax on benefits. It measures income_tax_chapter_1, chapter 1 liability after nonrefundable credits and before refundable credits. The share is that liability with section 86 inclusion as before the 1993 amendments, capped at actual inclusion, less the liability with no inclusion. At current law the cap changes nothing. When a reform cuts inclusion below the pre-1993 amount, the 1993 amendments add nothing, so OASDI takes the whole increase. The HI share, tob_revenue_medicare_hi, stays out. The share carries a sign: a unit whose tax falls when its benefits enter income contributes a negative amount, so sum units without a floor.

oasdi_niit_revenue is the OASDI transfer that the enabled S.770 section 8 options select. It is zero unless an option applies, and it counts only the transfer, not the whole net_investment_income_tax liability. See the S.770 section 8 README for the transfer modes.

Flows left out¶

  • employer_total_social_security_tax applies the employer rate to employer_total_taxable_earnings_for_social_security, a separate input for employer-only payroll tax calculations that defaults to zero, rather than to a worker’s own wages. Where data populate both, they describe the same employer liability, so the sum keeps only the worker-level employer_social_security_tax and the aggregate is not a fourth stream.

  • unreported_payroll_tax, the tax on unreported tips and wages from Forms 4137 and 8919, is a TaxUnit input that doesn’t separate OASDI from HI.

  • employer_ss_tax_income_tax_revenue, the income tax on employer OASDI contributions under the contributed reform that counts employer payroll tax as income, isn’t part of oasdi_income. A score that enables that reform adds it separately.

Benefit taxation and the NIIT transfer¶

tob_revenue_oasdi takes the difference in income_tax_chapter_1, which leaves out net_investment_income_tax. Including benefits raises adjusted gross income and so niit_magi, so benefit inclusion can trigger NIIT. The trust fund transfer covers only chapter 1 income tax liabilities, and the NIIT sits in chapter 2A, so that NIIT stays out of the benefit taxation term. With an S.770 section 8 option on, oasdi_niit_revenue transfers shares of the section 8 add-on and active taxes, and in bill or combined-rate mode of the existing-rate NIIT. Benefit inclusion can raise those taxes too. oasdi_income counts the transferred share of that increase once, in the NIIT transfer term.

Entity mapping and weights¶

oasdi_income credits each tax unit’s amount once, to the household of its first member. Microsimulation weights a tax unit by that same household’s weight, so household-weighted totals equal the Person and TaxUnit weighted totals. PE-US declares that tax units sit inside households, and the chunked helper below requires it. If data ever breaks that nesting, the first-member credit still keeps the weighted totals equal. Core’s group-to-group adds would instead split a tax unit’s amount equally among its members and credit each member’s share to that member’s household.

When you aggregate, sum each output at its own entity with that entity’s weights, once. Don’t project a tax-unit amount to its members and sum it again: calculate("oasdi_benefit_taxation_revenue", map_to="person").sum() counts a married couple’s amount twice.

Stated limitations¶

This page states these limitations without measuring them. No residual from another model closes them.

Missing cash flows. The modeled balance leaves out interest on trust fund reserves, the Railroad Retirement financial interchange, administrative expenses and general-fund reimbursements. None of these flows is a model variable.

Recipients outside the frame. oasdi_cost sums benefits for the people in the household dataset. The dataset misses or only partly represents beneficiaries outside its frame, such as those living abroad.

Noncovered employment. payroll_tax_gross_wages is employment income less FICA pre-tax contributions, with no general OASDI-coverage flag. The model therefore taxes wages from employment that OASDI doesn’t cover, such as some state and local government jobs, as covered wages. This wage-coverage assumption differs from the missing cash flows.

Pass-through classification in the scoring data. The owners’ draw term in payroll liability and oasdi_niit_revenue, in both its NII and active parts, depend on pass-through classification fields. The certified artifact’s stored columns don’t include passive_partnership_s_corp_income, which therefore defaults to zero. Population values for those two terms stay provisional until a scoring artifact carries the repaired fields and the preflight receipt confirms them; the S.770 section 8 README describes the repairs.

Scoring a population¶

  1. Audit the stored data first. Run the scoring-data preflight on the exact H5 you’ll score:

    python -m policyengine_us.tools.social_security_preflight /data/populace_us_2024.h5 > receipt.json

    It checks the artifact’s hash and base year and the four benefit components’ sources. It also rejects stored columns that would bypass these formulas: the six outputs above, aggregate social_security, the worker and employer OASDI tax leaves, the TOB revenue leaves and the income_tax_chapter_1 they difference, and the NIIT liability and transfer leaves, including the S.770 section 8 and owners’ draw tax and OASDI transfer leaves. Keep the receipt with the score.

  2. Score in household chunks. On main, tob_revenue_oasdi recalculates income tax in two branches of the whole simulation, so its working memory grows with the population. Household-chunked calculation, PolicyEngine/policyengine-us#10561, is the supported route for full-population scoring once it merges. Review may still change its interface, so check its documentation when it lands. Score the same file the preflight hashed. With the interface under review, a call looks like this:

    from policyengine_us import Microsimulation
from policyengine_us.tools.calculate_chunked import calculate_chunked

result = calculate_chunked(
    lambda dataset: Microsimulation(dataset=dataset, dataset_end_year=2035),
    [
        "oasdi_payroll_tax_revenue",
        "oasdi_benefit_taxation_revenue",
        "oasdi_niit_revenue",
        "oasdi_income",
        "oasdi_cost",
        "oasdi_modeled_balance",
    ],
    2030,
    8,
    dataset="/data/populace_us_2024.h5",
)
totals = result.weighted_sums

    The helper keeps households whole and requires each tax unit to sit inside one household, so each output’s chunk totals add to its population total.

  3. Check the identities on the totals. The oasdi_income total equals the payroll, benefit-taxation and NIIT-transfer totals added together, and the oasdi_modeled_balance total equals the oasdi_income total less the oasdi_cost total.

  4. Treat projected years as independent cross-sections. The preflight guide describes how extend_single_year_dataset projects each year from the base year. Report the limitations above with every score.
