Social Security COLA means test¶

The contributed COLA means test is disabled under current law. It can withhold an annual COLA based on the beneficiary’s tax unit, while retaining the benefit level reached before that event. It supports both reported benefit components and the supplied-record household calculator. Scenario thresholds and dates come from the scoring settings; PE-US does not choose package values.

Policy basis and choices¶

The income test follows OACT provision A9 on the 2026 Trustees Report basis. Its instruction is: “Use prior tax year income data for this determination.” The live page was available when this implementation was prepared.

A9 uses a chained CPI-U COLA below the income line and no COLA above it, for all beneficiaries beginning with the December 2028 COLA. Its single, head-of-household and separate filers share a line; joint filers have twice that line. It also specifies chained CPI-U before benefit receipt. Its example lines are \(85,000 and \)170,000 in 2018 dollars, indexed by CPI-U. The preset below implements these mechanics. Its 2029 income lines are \(117,800 single and \)235,700 joint. OACT’s displayed December 2028 lines of \(117,300 and \)234,600 use a different CPI projection; this model uses the authored CPI-U path described below.

The default enabled scenario differs from A9 in two parameterized ways: it retains the statutory COLA below the line and restricts the policy to people first eligible from 2026. The effective date, first-eligible year, filing-group mapping, dollar lines, base year, index lag and rounding increment are also parameters. A surviving spouse maps to the joint line by default; this is a modeling choice because A9 does not name that status. Only income strictly above the line withholds a COLA. Equality receives the COLA, an explicit convention for A9’s otherwise unspecified equality case.

means_test.enabled activates both the income decision and the selected index without requiring another switch. The enclosing cola.withholding_enabled remains the A5 switch for externally resolved withholding decisions when the means test is disabled. With the means test enabled, use the separate annual Person inputs social_security_X_cola_override and ss_calculator_cola_override: -1 means automatic (the default), 0 means paid, and 1 means withheld. Each person’s default remains automatic when another person supplies an override. The automatic timing, cohort and receipt restrictions apply to automatic decisions; an explicit override supplies the final decision for that person and year. The tri-state overrides expire after their supplied year; later years return to automatic decisions.

These controls do not establish income history for Medicare IRMAA’s lookback. With reform parameters at their defaults, supplying them changes no benefit, tax or Medicare premium. This exclusion is specific to the new controls; legacy component withholding inputs retain their existing history behavior. Dataset extension resets all five controls to -1 in generated future rows, while preserving decisions in every year the dataset actually supplied.

Core stores these inputs as integers: floats truncate first (for example, 0.5 becomes 0). Integers outside -1, 0 and 1 raise ValueError when an active exact-year decision reads them. A multi-year input such as year:2027:2 creates no exact annual decision in either year.

With the means test disabled, the reported route retains A5’s boolean social_security_X_cola_withheld inputs and their legacy carry-forward behavior. The calculator reads only ss_calculator_withhold_cola, in its explicitly supplied year. Component boolean inputs do not reach the calculator. These legacy boolean channels do not override an enabled means test; use the separate tri-state inputs for that purpose.

Positive single and joint lines are required when a prior_year income decision is evaluated. The never_withheld and always_withheld sensitivities and pre-receipt index-only events do not read those lines.

The a9 preset in policyengine_us.reforms.ssa.cola_means_test enables the policy and selects:

scope: 1  # all_beneficiaries
effective_year: 2028
effective_month: 12
below_threshold_cola_index: 1  # chained_cpi_u
pre_receipt_cola_index: 1  # chained_cpi_u
income_sensitivity: 0  # prior_year
thresholds:
  base_year: 2018
  single: 85000
  joint: 170000
  index_lag: 1
  rounding: 100

Apply it as a reform, for example:

from policyengine_us import Simulation
from policyengine_us.reforms.ssa.cola_means_test import a9

simulation = Simulation(situation=situation, reform=a9)

These are public A9 example settings, not a scenario’s package values. The first affected checks are in January 2029. The ordinary contributed scenario instead supplies its own positive 2026-dollar lines and retains the default statutory/cohort choices unless its policy specifies otherwise.

Prior-year income and filing status¶

social_security_cola_means_test_magi is a dedicated annual TaxUnit variable. Its definition is AGI less taxable Social Security, plus tax-exempt interest and the IRMAA-style exclusions under Internal Revenue Code sections 135, 911, 931 and 933. AGI retains its applicable deductions. This implements the required MAGI excluding Social Security, replacing the earlier gross-source income proposal. The A9-linked actuarial memorandum, provision 5 on page 8 connects the lines to Medicare IRMAA; H.R. 6489 section 103(d) supplies the corresponding IRMAA definition reference. Excluding Social Security and using the prior year are requirements of this contributed lever.

For COLAs first paid in year y, the decision reads this MAGI and filing status for tax year y - 1. It never substitutes current-year AGI. A supplied MAGI history can directly set the dedicated variable for the relevant tax years; supplied histories of its income sources and filing status also take precedence over projected records. A tax unit’s decision maps to each beneficiary, subject to that component’s exposure and cohort scope. Core stores complete input arrays for each variable and period; an income history column must supply a value for every tax unit in that array.

Although prior-year AGI can depend on prior-year Social Security, every benefit-related lookback moves to an earlier year. The dependency chain stops before the policy’s first affected event. It cannot return to the same benefit/year pair. Multi-year dependency-graph tests exercise this decreasing year chain, including deductions whose MAGI depends on taxable benefits. The household engine resolves decisions only through the requested check year for payment outputs. It does not eagerly read the income for the next January COLA while computing this year’s checks. December entitlement and annual entitlement can require that next event; their prior-income dependency uses the separate public payment route.

Population scoring uses a static-cross-section prior-income proxy. Dataset extension starts each year from the same original record and uprates its monetary sources separately. The y - 1 record is that same record’s extended prior-year copy, not an observed longitudinal income history. Ages, cohorts and household composition are not carried forward as a person’s history. Supplied histories override this proxy. Compare income_sensitivity = 1 (never_withheld) and 2 (always_withheld) to report the missing-income-history sensitivity envelope; both retain policy timing and cohort scope.

Threshold indexing and COLA construction¶

For filing group g, base year b, payment year y, index lag L and rounding increment R, the income line is:

line(g, y) = round_half_up_to_R(
    base_line(g) * CPI_U(December, y - L) / CPI_U(December, b)
)
withheld(y) = prior_year_MAGI > line(g, y)

The code reads gov.bls.cpi.cpi_u at December 1 of year y - L, and at December 1 of the base year. Under the parameter lookup’s ordinary semantics, this returns a BLS December figure where one exists, otherwise the latest authored value. The series currently contains BLS monthly observations through June 2026, so cpi_u("2026-12-01") returns June 2026’s 332.568. For later years it uses CBO’s February-keyed projection: for example, cpi_u("2028-12-01") returns 348.3, authored at 2028-02-01. These reads do not construct a separate December projection series.

The default one-year lag uses the December 1, 2028 lookup for January 2029 checks. The A9 preset’s \(85,000/\)170,000 base lines, December 2018 value 251.233, and projected value 348.3 give \(117,800/\)235,700 after nearest-\(100 half-up rounding. OACT's displayed \)117,300/\(234,600 lines use a different CPI projection. This December lookup is a scoring assumption: a single filer at \)117,500 falls between PolicyEngine’s and OACT’s lines. The December 1 lookup, one-year payment lag and rounding are documented modeling conventions: A9 specifies CPI-U indexing without prescribing these details. Pin both the authored index path and these settings for a score.

The chained option uses Q3 averages of July, August and September levels from gov.bls.cpi.c_cpi_u. As with the statutory CPI-W schedule, positive growth is rounded half up to 0.1 percentage point; nonpositive or rounded-zero growth gives no COLA. The comparison base is the Q3 associated with the last positive index-derived COLA. A withheld year still advances that index schedule’s comparison base when its rounded rate is positive: income withholding must not create a catch-up COLA later. Neither withholding nor a downward rate adjustment reduces the nominal benefit level. Historical benefit-rounding rules remain attached to their individual events.

Before receipt, pre_receipt_cola_index controls COLAs accrued since initial eligibility. Its default follows below_threshold_cola_index; the A9 preset therefore uses chained CPI-U for these events. From the policy’s effective start, these pre-receipt events use the chosen index without an income test. Supply entitlement and worker eligibility dates when known. Missing-date cohort and exposure approximations remain those described in the benefit-lever guide, including adjacent-cohort sensitivities for integer-age uncertainty.

An active A5 cola.alternative_rate overrides the index selected here. The applicable cola.pp_change is then added to that rate, and the final rate is floored at zero. Withholding overrides the entire resulting increase.

One joint COLA adjustment¶

Each annual Person variable social_security_X_cola_means_test_withheld, for X equal to retirement, disability, survivors or dependents, resolves its tax unit’s automatic decision and that person’s separate social_security_X_cola_override input. The legacy social_security_X_cola_withheld boolean remains A5’s input channel when the means test is disabled. For a reported benefit, the population adjustment accumulates one ratio for each exposed event:

event numerator = 1                         if withheld
                  1 + adjusted_COLA        otherwise
event ratio     = event numerator / (1 + statutory_COLA)
joint factor    = product of the exposed event ratios

The adjusted rate includes the selected index and applicable percentage-point change, with a floor of zero. For example, statutory rates of 2% and 3% plus a CPI-E-style addition of 0.15 percentage points give numerators 1.0215 and 1.0315 if paid. If the second year is withheld, the two-year factor is (1.0215 / 1.02) * (1 / 1.03), approximately 0.97230. The reformed level from a pre-event amount of \(1,000 is \)1,021.50, compared with the statutory $1,050.60. The withheld event receives no CPI-E increase. An independently multiplied withholding factor would give the wrong result.

The household calculator applies the same per-year decisions inside its COLA schedule. It rounds the resulting PIA under the applicable event rule and applies claiming and payment calculations once. With the means test enabled, the annual ss_calculator_cola_override takes precedence over the component override, followed by the automatic decision. With the means test disabled, it retains A5’s exact-year ss_calculator_withhold_cola channel.

For a public retirement component, select social_security_retirement_source as supplied_aime or supplied_pia, and supply the matching ss_calculator_source of aime or pia. For a restricted survivor record, select social_security_survivors_source = supplied_ratio_survivor and ss_calculator_source = survivor. These routes retain the calculator’s input and scope requirements in the calculator guide.

Calculated public routes bypass all population benefit factors: the engine already applies the COLA, across-the-board and payable policies to its ledger. Reported sources use the population factors. This avoids applying any of these policies twice. Switching a policy off later does not restore an earlier withheld increase.

Validation contract¶

Validation covers all statutory/chained and cohort/all-beneficiary combinations, both sides of the first-eligibility boundary, the A9 preset, all filing statuses, equality, supplied histories, and both income sensitivities. Boundary cases include a zero statutory COLA, negative rate adjustments, high-water years and combined CPI-E changes and withholding. Multi-year graph checks establish that the prior-year income read has no cycle. Calculated-source cases check that the COLA policy is applied once.

Compatibility checks pin literal results from main for disabled-policy reported and calculator routes, including A5 inputs. Other checks require nonincreasing benefits when a year is withheld and monotone withholding as income rises at a fixed line. Multi-person cases preserve each person’s automatic decision when someone else supplies an override. Differential checks cover reported situations, downstream outputs and household calculator examples with this means test disabled. Population impact requires a separately pinned scoring run; these mechanics do not substitute OACT’s solvency estimates for a PE-US result.
