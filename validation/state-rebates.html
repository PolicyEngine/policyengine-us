State rebate attribution¶

PolicyEngine attributes one-time state rebates to the tax year whose return determines eligibility and amount, even when checks arrive in a later calendar year. Rebates included in state credit totals reduce state_income_tax and increase household net income in that eligibility year. This attribution convention does not describe the payment calendar. Some standalone rebate formulas can calculate an amount after the rebate ends; use the dated credit total to establish whether that amount enters tax or net income. Do not add back every positive raw rebate column in every year. Massachusetts premium pay is also modeled through state refundable credits. A payment modeled solely as a benefit would affect household net income without reducing state income tax.

For comparison, restore only the rebate amounts that actually reduced that year’s reported state tax, including liability limits on nonrefundable credits. Ordinary recurring refundable credits remain part of liability. state_income_tax_before_refundable_credits removes those ordinary credits too, so it is not a pre-rebate liability measure. Colorado Cash Back and the residual TABOR sales tax refund are separate mechanisms; count each only through its stated aggregation path and eligibility year.

Inventory¶

The years below are eligibility tax years in which a qualifying household can receive a nonzero contribution to the state’s credit total. R denotes the state’s refundable-credit total; N denotes its nonrefundable-credit total. Every listed payment enters state tax through credits, including Colorado Cash Back and Massachusetts premium pay. None is a second, direct household benefit payment. Refundable totals enter both state tax and household refundable credits; nonrefundable totals reduce tax before refundable credits.

Standalone amounts and comparison measures¶

Illinois’s two rebates, New Mexico’s three rebates, and Hawaii’s Act 115 refund can return positive standalone amounts after 2021 because their schedules persist. Their historical guards make the amounts zero before 2021, and the dated credit totals exclude them after 2021. Arizona and Massachusetts Chapter 62F also have standalone formulas that can remain positive in other years, while their dated credit lists include them only in 2021.

Maine, Montana, South Carolina, Colorado Cash Back, Connecticut, Rhode Island, Georgia, New York, and Massachusetts premium pay have amount or formula end dates. Eligibility helpers and positive standalone amounts are not substitutes for checking the dated aggregation path and applied liability cap.

The TAXSIM validation comparison includes a rebate-adjusted metric. Its limitations and each program’s aggregation path matter when reconciling that metric with the reported state income tax.
