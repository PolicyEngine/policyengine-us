Social Security annual earnings histories¶

The opt-in household calculator accepts an annual covered-earnings history with ss_calculator_source: history. It computes conventional AIME and the initial eligibility PIA, with optional owners’ draw credit, annual mini-PIAs, and a separate above-cap AIME+ component. All reforms are off by default. Insured status is supplied explicitly; this route does not calculate credits or disability, family, survivor, or minimum benefits.

The existing supplied-AIME, supplied-PIA, and restricted-survivor routes remain available. Calculated ss_calculated_* outputs remain separate from reported public Social Security benefits, following the merged household calculator’s interface. The history selector does not replace a public benefit input.

Annual source and missing years¶

ss_calculator_earnings_history is one JSON string containing the complete source record. Keeping the career in one structural input preserves all years and supplied decimal amounts through source extraction and reload. It also avoids treating Core’s carry-forward of an annual scalar as a new earnings observation.

The JSON object contains:

Field

Meaning

first_year

First year enumerated by the history.

records

Annual observations with year, covered_wages, and covered_self_employment. Both monetary fields are required, including explicit zero.

certified_zero_years

Explicitly certified zero years omitted from records.

certified_zero_before_first_year

Certifies all earlier potential base years as zero. Required when first_year is later than the worker’s birth year or 1951, whichever is later.

Covered wages mean earnings in covered employment before the ordinary annual cap. Existing covered self-employment means earnings after their existing net-earnings adjustment, before the benefit cap. These are earnings amounts, not tax liabilities. Amounts may be JSON numbers or decimal strings; strings are useful when another application’s JSON encoder would lose precision.

Every year from first_year through the year before initial entitlement must be observed or certified zero. Missing years are rejected. Duplicate records, overlapping observations and zero certificates, nonfinite amounts, and negative covered earnings are rejected. Later observations may remain in the source but do not enter the initial calculation: a claim in year Q uses earnings through Q−1. Requesting a later benefit year does not recompute the initial earnings record.

For example, a worker born in 1964, first eligible in 2026 and claiming in 2029 can supply the following JSON string. The certificate is an explicit assertion about this synthetic worker’s earlier earnings:

{
  "first_year": 2026,
  "certified_zero_before_first_year": true,
  "certified_zero_years": [2027],
  "records": [
    {
      "year": 2026,
      "covered_wages": "60000.00",
      "covered_self_employment": "0.00"
    },
    {
      "year": 2028,
      "covered_wages": "65000.00",
      "covered_self_employment": "0.00"
    }
  ]
}

The calculator also requires the existing own-record fields: worker ID, exact birth date, age-62 eligibility year, first entitlement month, insured-status assertion, and assertions about unsupported special earnings tests and prior withholding adjustments. The supported eligibility benchmark begins in 1984. Earnings can precede that benchmark, beginning in 1951 or the worker’s birth year, whichever is later; annual inactive-policy defaults cover those years.

The route computes only the wage-indexed formula of 42 U.S.C. 415(a)(1). For a worker with covered earnings before 1979 who first becomes eligible from 1979 through 1983, 415(a)(4)(B)(i) keeps the computation under subsection (a) as in effect in December 1978 when that amount is larger. The route cannot compute that amount, so it refuses eligibility years before 1984. At any eligibility year, 415(a)(4)(B)(ii) also keeps the subsection (d) computation when it is larger. Subsection (d) computes the 1939 Act primary insurance benefit, which counts wages before 1951, and 415(d)(2)(A) limits it to a worker with a quarter of coverage before 1951. Annual records begin in 1951, so the route cannot take those wages or compute that amount, and it assumes the worker had no covered wages before 1951. It also assumes the worker had no disability-benefit eligibility before January 1979, the case 415(a)(4)(A) takes outside paragraph (1).

Conventional AIME and the shared benefit pipeline¶

For eligibility year E and initial claim year Q:

  1. Include annual earnings through Q−1, including earnings after age 62.

  2. Cap each year’s covered wages plus existing covered SE plus enabled owners’ draw credit at that earnings year’s ordinary contribution and benefit base.

  3. For a year y before E−2, multiply by NAWI(E−2) / NAWI(y) and round to cents, with half cents rounded upward. Earnings in E−2 and later remain at face value.

  4. Select the cohort’s computation years: min(35, E − 1956), so 28 years for E = 1984, 34 for E = 1990 and 35 from E = 1991. Select that many highest annual indexed amounts, pad with certified zeros, divide by that count times 12 (336, 408 or 420 months in those examples), and floor to a whole monthly dollar.

  5. Call the same eligibility-year compute_pia service used by supplied AIME.

Step 4 follows 42 U.S.C. 415(b)(2): elapsed years run from 1951 (or the year after age 21, if later) through the year before age-62 eligibility, and the computation period is elapsed years less five, with a minimum of two (POMS RS 00605.016 and RS 00605.017). A worker first eligible in 1990 has 39 elapsed years and 34 computation years. For example, \(3,000 of covered wages in every year 1951–1989 with eligibility and claim in 1990 indexes to a highest-34 sum of \)372,486.21, so AIME is floor(372,486.21 / 408) = \(912 and the PIA is \)498.30; a fixed 35-year, 420-month selection would give \(895 and \)492.80. Legacy ss_aime keeps its own window, and the supplied-AIME and supplied-PIA routes are unchanged.

The POMS indexing procedure establishes the index year and cent rounding. The POMS AIME/PIA procedure specifies whole-dollar AIME and lower-dime PIA rounding for modern benchmarks. The initial base-year rules distinguish the initial calculation from later recomputations. This calculator deliberately freezes earnings at initial claim; post-claim recomputation is outside this deliverable.

The household payment service then applies eligibility-origin COLAs, the claiming adjustment, contributed payment policies, rounding, and the earnings test once. The SSA COLA application sequence truncates the PIA after a COLA and the claiming-adjusted amount to dimes, followed by the applicable final payment rounding.

An entitlement month’s check is normally paid the following month. Annual entitlement and annual payments are separate totals. A January first entitlement therefore normally produces 12 entitlement months and 11 checks in that calendar year.

Owners’ draw credit¶

When E1 is enabled for an observed earnings year, its record must contain owners_draw with all four signed fields: s_corp_income, partnership_income, passive_income, and already_se_income, including explicit zero values. Include subsets_are_disjoint or passive_se_overlap when required. Certified-zero years supply zero owners’ inputs automatically. The passive and already-SE amounts are subsets already included in the entity totals. already_se_income is a gross partnership source amount; it differs from the record’s adjusted covered_self_employment.

The history route and installed reform call one shared compute_owners_draw kernel. Extracting the existing arithmetic preserves the reform’s intermediate outputs and input overrides while avoiding a second implementation. Annual policy parameters govern activation, the phase-in share, netting, the separate reporting floor, and wages/SE/draw room priority. See the owners’ draw specification for signed losses and overlapping subsets.

The shared kernel retains the reform’s existing floating-point and NumPy intermediate arithmetic. The history adapter restores its monetary outputs with Decimal(str(...)), then applies the history route’s annual indexed-cent rounding. This preserves the existing reform computation while keeping the subsequent annual earnings ledger separate from Core’s float32 input storage.

The default netting expression is 1 − employer_share × (SE_OASDI_rate + SE_HI_rate), or 0.9235 at current rates. The planned adjusted draw must independently meet the $400 net-earnings floor at current settings. Enabled benefit credit adds accepted adjusted draw N_D to annual covered earnings before the ordinary cap. It does not append N_D to AIME or derive earnings by dividing a tax amount by a rate.

The room_cap_basis parameter preserves the current-law cap or explicitly removes the draw ceiling. It changes the tax-side room; ordinary benefit credit still follows the annual benefit cap and any separately enabled above-threshold credit. The existing reform’s active NIIT exclusion remains draw_oasdi_base / q with OASDI only and the full actual gross draw with HI.

Mini-PIA¶

The named compute_mini_pia function applies the same eligibility-year PIA formula to each annual ordinary indexed covered amount divided by 12. It selects the highest 40 annual mini-PIAs, pads shorter certified careers with zeros, and divides their sum by 37. The divisor is always 37.

The archived B3.12 description in OACT’s 2025 Trustees Report edition says, “Sum these individual PIAs for the 40 highest years of indexed earnings and divide that total amount by 37”. Its retired-worker phase-in, in that 2025 edition, is 20% in 2032, 40% in 2033, 60% in 2034, 80% in 2035, and 100% from 2036. Disability benefits are unchanged. The independently enabled parameter path is under gov.contrib.ssa.history.mini_pia.

Modeling convention: combined dime rounding. Retain precision in each mini-AIME and mini-PIA, aggregate with /37, and blend mini_share_E × mini_PIA + (1 − mini_share_E) × conventional_PIA. Conventional AIME/PIA retains its statutory rounding. Add the separate AIME+ PIA after the blend, then floor the combined eligibility PIA to a dime once. 42 U.S.C. 415(a)(1)(A) rounds the summed formula result down to a multiple of \(0.10; the provision descriptions do not address rounding. For example, a mini of \)399.99 plus a \(0.02 credit gives a \)400 PIA and payment; truncating the mini first would give \(399.90 and \)399.

Above-cap AIME+ credit¶

Independent named parameter sets under gov.contrib.ssa.history.donut retain the archived oact_2025_e2_13 and oact_2025_e2_14 provisions and a custom set. Activation, threshold, start year, tax rate, credit flag/rate, and inclusion of existing SE or reclassified draw are separate settings. A credit-off variant can tax earnings while producing no AIME+ credit.

The archived payroll-tax descriptions in OACT’s 2025 Trustees Report edition state E2.13 as “Apply OASDI 12.4 percent payroll tax rate on earnings above \(400,000” and E2.14 as “Apply OASDI 12.4 percent payroll tax rate on earnings above \)250,000”. In that 2025 edition both begin with 2027 earnings and use a 2% secondary AIME+ factor. The 2026 Trustees Report edition moved the start of E2.13 and E2.14 to 2028. This calculator keeps the 2025 edition’s years and citations; its named variants (oact_2025_e2_13, oact_2025_e2_14) and the B3.12 phase-in above are the 2025 edition’s. Their thresholds stay fixed; once the ordinary cap reaches the threshold, the gap disappears.

For each enabled earnings year, the separate taxed stream is max(selected_covered_earnings − max(ordinary_cap, threshold), 0). The gap between cap and threshold receives neither additional tax nor credit. Earnings receive AIME+ credit only when the provision taxes them and enables credit. Including existing SE or adjusted owners’ draw applies consistently to the tax and credit ledger.

Index the extra stream using the same E−2 convention, select its own highest 35 years, divide by 420 and floor to whole dollars. Multiply that separate AIME+ by the credit rate and add it after the mini/conventional blend. This stream never changes conventional AIME. These services expose hypothetical annual tax/credit amounts for the household report; they do not change population payroll-tax variables.

Modeling convention: cohort-selected donut coefficient. For a custom changing path, annual tax/coverage settings determine the extra earnings stream, and the PIA credit coefficient is read at eligibility E, as benefit formulas select by cohort. The named E2.13 and E2.14 variants (2025 Trustees Report edition) carry a constant 2%, so this only moves a custom path that varies. For example, AIME+ \(240 at 2% against 3% gives \)4.80 against \(7.20; with a \)2,180 baseline PIA and claiming at 65, payment is \(1,893 against \)1,895. Pure callers whose credited annual streams use different coefficients must supply the eligibility coefficient explicitly.

Annual earnings ledger¶

AnnualBenefit.history exposes the typed history calculation to Python callers. ss_calculated_history_ledger exposes its annual ledger as a JSON string for a household report. The ledger retains ordinary covered earnings, accepted E1 credit, the separate donut-taxed and credited stream, indexing amounts, and each year’s mini-PIA so the result can be reconciled by year.

The accompanying annual diagnostics are ss_calculated_history_aime, ss_calculated_history_aime_plus, ss_calculated_history_mini_pia, and ss_calculated_history_conventional_pia. These diagnostics preserve the initial-claim earnings calculation when a later payment year is requested. ss_calculator_donut_variant selects the named donut parameter set; its default custom set is inactive until explicitly configured.

The annual report prices mini-PIAs and PIA diagnostics using January entitlement’s formula; diagnostic_pia_entitlement_month and diagnostic_pia_formula_year name that period. donut_credit_rate (with its exact fraction) and donut_credit_rate_year name the AIME+ coefficient applied and the year whose parameters selected it. computation_years gives the ordinary selection count, which the ordinary_top35 flags follow; the donut_top35 flags always mark 35 years. Its income ledger stays frozen at Q−1. An exact fraction accompanies recurring mini-PIA decimals, and highest-year selection flags use the earlier earnings year to resolve ties. Before eligibility, the report retains the earnings ledger and leaves PIA diagnostics unset.

Reviewable arithmetic and compatibility¶

The synthetic 2036-eligibility fixture uses flat NAWI, zero COLAs, a \(120,000 ordinary cap, \)1,000/\(6,000 bend points, and .90/.32/.15 factors. Thirty-three \)60,000 wage years and a \(120,000 capped year give AIME \)5,000 and PIA \(2,180.00. A \)60,000 accepted gross draw contributes \(55,410, giving AIME \)5,131 and conventional PIA \(2,221.90. A \)500,800 wage year contributes \(100,800 to the \)400,000 donut stream: AIME+ \(240 and PIA+ \)4.80.

Annual minis are \(2,180.00 for a \)60,000 year, \(3,100.00 for the capped year, and \)2,057.60 for the owners’ year. The mini with E1 is (33 × 2180 + 3100 + 2057.60) / 37 = 2083.718918…. Adding PIA+ and truncating to dimes gives \(2,088.50. Claiming 24 months before FRA uses 13/15 and pays \)1,810 monthly, or $21,720 for 12 entitlement months.

Published conventional comparisons use SSA’s annual earnings histories and PIA examples. In the 2027 examples, Case A has AIME \(6,072; SSA prints the formula result \)2,720.24 and then the initial PIA \(2,720.20. Case B, first eligible in 2022, has AIME \)11,843, including later earnings through 2026; SSA’s page prints the formula result \(3,419.61, and \)3,419.60 is its floor to a dime, which the calculator reports as the initial PIA. Source data are fixed in the corresponding test fixtures; the live SSA examples can advance to another retirement year.

The merged implementation takes precedence over older design descriptions:

  • The existing ss_calculated_* interface remains separate from reported public benefits. Payment-year totals retain the merged one-month check lag.

  • Runtime parameters already include historical bends and future wage-indexed paths. The history route reuses them, including future household claim years.

  • Legacy ss_aime keeps its rolling lookback, age filters, and untruncated result. The opt-in complete-history route shares applicable arithmetic and is compared with it where those legacy assumptions match.

  • One structural JSON source holds all annual observations instead of many period-specific inputs. Explicit certificates preserve missing-versus-zero semantics and make source export complete.

There are no package-specific settings, population impact estimates, insured credits algorithm, or post-claim earnings recomputations in this interface.
