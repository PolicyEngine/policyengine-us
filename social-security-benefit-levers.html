Social Security benefit levers¶

The benefit lever interface separates reported annual benefits from public benefit outputs. Stable public formulas combine a selected reported source with independent across-the-board and supplied payable-share factors, and one joint payment-year COLA factor. All factors are one by default. A stored-data preflight checks the scoring artifact’s identity and columns before population scoring.

The engine contract does not certify API or application field exposure. Calculated benefit source modes are reserved for subsequent implementations. No package-specific values are part of this interface.

Input and override contract¶

The public components are social_security_retirement, social_security_disability, social_security_survivors, and social_security_dependents. Their _reported twins are pure annual Person inputs, including zero, with the original effective per-capita SOI/CBO uprating. Public formulas have no variable-level uprating. Dataset extension still uprates original column names before constructor migration.

Initial ingress through either PE-US constructor, dataset loading, or Core’s SimulationBuilder (including YAML) migrates a legacy public input to its reported twin separately for each supplied period. The public formula then applies the levers in the stored year as well as later years. When both names are supplied in the same period, the reported amount remains the source and the public input remains a final override.

A public set_input after construction is a final override for its own period. It also seeds or refreshes the reported twin for that period unless the caller explicitly supplied that twin. Automatically migrated or seeded twins follow later public writes, preserving main’s uprating and earlier-year lookbacks; calculated or uprated caches do not block seeding. An explicitly supplied reported input, including zero, retains precedence. Aggregate social_security inputs remain final overrides. Clones and branches retain these rules. Later public writes replace their runtime input-name list privately, preserving overrides in legacy extraction without changing a parent or sibling clone. Migrated inputs extract as twins; later overrides extract alongside their twins and retain precedence on reload. A private provenance envelope preserves the distinction between automatic and explicit sources through legacy extraction and complete snapshots, including subsequent public writes after reload. Builders consume this metadata before validating ordinary situation inputs.

This compatibility layer requires Core 3.33.1 or later for recorded-input, storage-origin and replay APIs. The lockfile uses Core 3.33.2.

Each component has a _source selector with default reported. Reserved options supplied_aime, supplied_pia, supplied_ratio_survivor, and history fail explicitly until their calculation routes are implemented. Selection never depends on whether an amount is positive. The selected _base and the _pia_ratio_factor (currently one) are separate from reported inputs. Future calculated routes must bypass population PIA and COLA factors, apply the other factors once, and perform their earnings test on the resulting payable amount.

policyengine_us.tools.social_security_inputs.export_source_inputs(simulation) exports all recorded pure-input periods and entity memberships as sources, with supplied formula-variable values in a separate overrides payload. Cached calculations are excluded. reload_source_inputs(payload, ...) restores sources before final overrides. Core’s legacy extract_person retains every recorded input period for the four benefit components and their reported twins, excluding formula caches. This keeps later overrides final on reload and preserves their source histories and future uprating. Other variables keep Core’s usual first known period. Neither interface settles the separate API/application metadata and field-exposure contract.

Annual benefit inputs retain Core’s period normalization. For example, supplying social_security_retirement: {"2024-07": 500} through a PE-US constructor stores 500 under the rolling annual period year:2024-07, then migrates that exact stored period to _reported. A July monthly output still follows Core’s normal calendar-year amount divided by twelve; it is not a new monthly entitlement schedule. The complete source snapshot preserves the rolling annual key.

PE-US wrappers also preserve an exact supplied multi-year annual total, such as year:2024:2. They apply a benefit multiplier only when it is identical in every calendar year covered by that total. Differing annual multipliers require separately supplied annual source amounts, because the total provides no annual allocation for nonzero rows. Zero rows remain zero. An exact supplied _base amount takes precedence over _reported, and base neutralization or abolition still applies; base overrides for only part of a multi-year source require annual source amounts. Explicit public multi-year inputs remain final overrides, and neutralization still takes precedence. Raw Core SimulationBuilder and YAML should supply canonical annual _reported periods for lever calculations; the additional exact multi-year reported-source adapter belongs to PE-US wrappers. A weighted Microsimulation request also needs an explicit weight for that exact multi-year period, matching Core.

Default

Meaning

9999

across_the_board.rate

0

Proportional change, finite and at least -1

payable.share

1

Externally supplied annual share in [0,1]

payable.oasi_only

false

cola.start_year

9999

First affected January payment year

cola.effective_year

0

Optional first affected COLA effective year

cola.effective_month

12

Effective month when effective year is supplied

cola.end_year

0

Last affected payment year, or no end

cola.pp_change

0

Addition to the annual proportional COLA rate

cola.alternative_rate

-1

Supplied pinned alternative annual rate; -1 selects baseline

cola.withholding_enabled

false

Enable component payment-year withholding terms

A positive effective_year takes precedence over start_year: December 2028 means the first affected check is January 2029. Parameter values can vary by payment year. Example modeling assumptions are CPI-E pp_change = 0.0015 (+0.15 percentage points), or chained CPI pp_change = -0.0030 (-0.30 percentage points), with an explicitly selected start. These are examples, not observed index series or package presets. An alternative-rate path must be pinned and recorded by its supplier; it is not generated from a household.

For each exposed payment event, statutory baseline rate c and configured addition delta give adjusted rate max(0,c+delta), without a second rounding of the policy addition. The joint multiplier is (1+adjusted)/(1+c) unless that event is withheld, in which case it is 1/(1+c). Each component’s _cola_withheld term defaults false; a subsequent means-test implementation supplies the rules. Switching a policy off later preserves its earlier effect on the benefit level. Public reported-route benefits multiply this joint factor, the PIA ratio factor, the across-the-board factor, and the payable factor once.

The payable supplier specifies an annual path beginning in the depletion year and supplies a blended share for a depletion year beginning partway through the year. The engine does not predict depletion, share paths, or within-year fund cash flow. In OASI-only mode, disability is unaffected; a positive dependent benefit requires an explicit social_security_dependents_fund of oasi or di. Mixed or unidentified dependent streams require an external allocation/blended scenario; the engine does not assign the entire stream to OASI silently.

COLA history and exposure¶

Historical statutory events are under gov.ssa.cola_history through December 2025 (January 2026 payment), including June/July events before 1983, zero-rate years and the corrected December 1999 2.5% rate. The current main January 2027 SSA schedule already includes the actual Q3 2026 index. Starting with that schedule entry, rate construction uses gov.ssa.uprating, comparing with the Q3 index associated with the last effective nonzero COLA. Decimal half-up rounding to 0.1 percentage point preserves the high-water base over zero-COLA years. cola_events(system.parameters, through_year) in tools.social_security_benefit_levers exports the exact baseline schedule for a scoring receipt. Freeze this schedule together with the reform parameters.

Historical PIA COLA rounding is implemented separately by tools.ssa_cola_history.apply_cola_to_pia: round upward to a dime before June 1982 and downward thereafter; unknown rule codes fail explicitly. Reported-route ratios validate exposed rules but do not reconstruct a rounded individual PIA from an annual recipient amount. Initial-PIA computations/inverses before the 1983 benchmark remain outside this implementation. Bend-point history is now available back to 1979 for future services.

Per-component optional inputs are _first_cola_year/_first_cola_month, _worker_eligibility_year, _worker_id, and _entitlement_year/_entitlement_month. A first-COLA date takes precedence. A worker link follows an explicit component basis on the linked worker, falling back to the shared authoritative social_security_worker_computation_year. A supplied recipient basis is preserved; conflicting positive bases or invalid links fail. No disability or deceased-worker computation basis is inferred from a linked worker’s retirement age.

Absent identified dates, own retirement uses age-62 eligibility (even if claiming later); older survivors use an age-60 recipient proxy. Positive disability, child/caregiver dependents and young survivors use a pre-start exposure proxy. These proxies are assumptions, not observed histories. Supply current-year entitlement/first-COLA dates as the lower-exposure sensitivity; compare adjacent retirement eligibility years for integer-age birthday uncertainty and age-50 survivor or linked-record alternatives where relevant. Report these sensitivity envelopes separately from identified results. Without worker/share information, auxiliary PIA inversion remains unidentified.

Each projected cross-section infers eligibility anew from year - age + 62. It does not carry cohorts or income histories across static population rows. A supplied retirement entitlement/claim date does not erase pre-claim COLAs. Pre-1983 retirement eligibility can expose a July payment in its eligibility year; modern retirement eligibility first exposes January of the next year.

Run the stored-data preflight described in the scoring-data guide before population lever scoring. Its receipt records the artifact, bytes hash, stored year, full stored columns, source mapping and projection convention.

For a supplied alternative-index level path, convert its pinned levels (including an explicit pre-start anchor) with projected_cola_rates, then supply the resulting payment-year rates under cola.alternative_rate. Record the source, anchor, level path and generated rate path with the scoring receipt.
