Court supervision and child-care subsidies¶

Policy review: September 28, 2026. Scope: the child-care subsidy programs modeled by PolicyEngine US, across the 50 states and DC.

45 CFR 98.20(a)(1)(ii) makes the older-child court-supervision route a state option. The correct national implementation therefore includes negative cases. Court supervision, a court order directing child care, and a protective-services agency referral or approval are separate facts in law. Court status never establishes disability.

is_under_court_supervision is the only input: an annual person boolean, default false. Where a state’s court-related rule requires a court order or case plan with specific content (directing child care, constant supervision or constant care), that content is treated as a verification detail and the rule is modeled through court supervision, as is_disabled stands in for verified incapacity. Court status does not establish an agency referral, authorization or waiver; a financial waiver or rate applies only where the state rule attaches it to the court category itself. New formulas are limited to child care.

Microsimulation limitation: no dataset populates is_under_court_supervision, so it is false for every record. In microsimulation, no court-supervised teenager qualifies through these routes, and the DC and South Carolina court waivers never apply. Results are biased downward by an unmeasured, likely small amount. Reforms to the court-supervision parameters score $0.

Routes not modeled¶

  • Texas: the court-related route is DFPS-authorized and funded protective child care (40 TAC 809.49), an agency authorization. The formula is unchanged, and tests confirm court supervision alone has no effect.

  • Delaware and New Jersey: DFS and CP&P protective-care referrals, and their payment and copay treatment, are agency routes separate from court supervision.

  • South Carolina: income and activity waivers for protective services are case-by-case (plan 2.2.2(g)–(h)).

  • Iowa: care directed in a court order is provided only “in certain cases” at the department’s discretion.

These changes retain existing disability proxies, annual-age approximations, and enrollment/renewal limitations unless expressly addressed above. Generic protective-services inputs also retain their existing meaning; court supervision must not be inferred from them. This is not an assertion that every protective-services pathway or every separate TANF/foster-care program is fully modeled.

Maryland uses the approved plan¶

The approved FFY2025–27 plan, §2.2.1(c) elects the court-supervision extension through age 18. The official current COMAR 13A.14.06.02B(11),(16) instead defines an older eligible child through disability. The agency’s public eligibility guidance also describes the disability route. The plan checkbox was checked visually; the discrepancy is not an extraction ambiguity.

The Maryland implementation follows the FFY2022–24 and FFY2025–27 plans from October 1, 2021; the FFY2019–21 plan elected No. It documents the differing COMAR definition. This source choice does not treat the plan as a regulatory amendment or infer any financial/activity waiver.

New Jersey and Delaware use the approved plan¶

The New Jersey FFY2025–27 Initial Plan (certified September 27, 2024; approved November 9, 2024), §2.2.1(c), elects the court-supervision extension through age 18. N.J.A.C. 10:15-1.3(a) instead lists the under-13 and under-19 disabled children and the SSI, Title IV-E and N.J.A.C. 10:90-2.7 dependent-child cases, with no court route. The approved Delaware FFY2025–27 plan, §2.2.1(c) (approved November 9, 2024) makes the same election; the DSSM handbook describes only the under-13, under-19 disabled and DFS-referral routes.

Both states also elected the extension through age 18 in the FFY2019–21 plans (NJ, DE) and FFY2022–24 plans (NJ, DE). New Jersey’s FFY2016–18 plan also elected it (NJ), while Delaware’s declined it (DE). Following the Maryland treatment of dating the route from the plan elections, the court_supervision_extension parameter is true from June 1, 2016 in New Jersey and from October 1, 2018 in Delaware; earlier periods follow the FFY2016–18 plans. The court route uses the existing under-19 special-needs ceiling and keeps the ordinary income, activity, immigration and copay rules; it does not create a CP&P or DFS referral.
