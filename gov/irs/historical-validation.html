Historical federal tax validation¶

The federal tax reference tests compare PolicyEngine with Tax-Calculator 6.9.1 for six hypothetical filing units in every year from 2017 through 2026. All 60 cases agree within $0.01 on eight outputs: adjusted gross income, standard deduction, personal exemptions, taxable income, income tax before credits, EITC, income tax after credits, and employee payroll tax. The integration file contains these cases alongside two existing tests.

Household coverage and assumptions¶

Each year uses the same wages, ages and family composition. These examples do not age a cohort or use survey records.

The head is 35 and the spouse, where present, is 33. The cases assume full receipt of eligible credits, wages as the only income source, and no itemized expenses. PolicyEngine uses Texas and explicitly sets ACA premium tax credits and state sales tax to zero. The comparison covers federal taxes only.

Tax-Calculator uses the policy year as the records’ start year, with no growth factors, weights or adjustment ratios. Its exact_calculations=True option preserves statutory steps rather than smoothing them. It does not reproduce every IRS Tax Table, EITC table or filing-rounding convention. The income-tax mapping uses soi_iitax=False; employee payroll tax equals ptax_was / 2 + ptax_amc, because ordinary wage FICA includes both shares while Additional Medicare Tax falls on the employee. The generator enforces these assumptions against the pinned Tax-Calculator source.

For 2020 and 2021, income tax includes annual Recovery Rebate Credit entitlement. These examples do not subtract advance payments and therefore do not represent the balance due or refund on a filed return.

Reproducing the independent reference¶

From the repository root, run the standalone generator in a separate environment:

uv run --no-project --isolated --with taxcalc==6.9.1 python \
  policyengine_us/tools/taxsim/generate_federal_tax_history_reference.py \
  --output federal-tax-history-reference.json

This command keeps Tax-Calculator out of the model’s environment. The generator imports no PolicyEngine runtime, requires a new JSON output path, checks the Tax-Calculator version and five source-file hashes, and records all synthetic inputs, outputs and dependency versions. It also rejects gaps in 2017–2026 coverage. Tooling tests check these guards.

Disposition of the 35 date flags¶

A frozen 2017 parameter trace identified 35 IRS parameter paths whose available histories began after 2017. A later first date alone does not establish an incorrect tax calculation: a constant can remain valid, a later provision can have an effective-date gate, and a parent node can overlap its leaf. The table accounts for all 35 paths. It does not count distinct laws or estimate the share of tax law that the model implements.

Every path below has the prefix gov.irs.. “Explicit 2017” means the parameter now has a source-linked 2017 entry; it does not necessarily mean its calculated value changed.

Additional rule tests and limits¶

Focused tests also cover the personal-exemption phaseout: a partial step triggers the full 2% reduction, as the 2017 worksheet, p. 41 requires. Dependent-care tests cover the actual qualified-expense ceiling and prevent reuse of excluded employer benefits for the child and dependent care credit, following Form 2441, Part III. Traditional IRA tests cover the age-70.5 restriction through 2019 and its removal from 2020, following Publication 590-A and P.L. 116-94, Division O, §107.

Employer dependent care assistance requires an independently supplied annual tax-unit input, dependent_care_assistance_qualified_expenses. It represents employment-related expenses for qualifying care incurred during the tax year, regardless of when paid, before employer benefits and statutory dollar or earnings limits. Include eligible employer-paid care and care for a qualifying disabled adult or spouse; exclude costs for ineligible people, purposes or providers and reimbursements disallowed under the applicable year’s rules. The 2017 instructions, p. 1 retain state reimbursements included in income; the shorter reimbursement wording in the 2025 instructions, p. 2 does not by itself establish a legal change. The input must reflect the applicable treatment.

When callers omit the expense input, the model uses zero and treats all positive employer benefits as taxable. That reporting assumption does not establish that the household incurred no qualified costs. An informed household or population calculation requires an independently established qualified amount; W-2 box 10, gross childcare bills and modeled public subsidies do not supply it. Both the 2017 and 2025 instructions illustrate \(2,000 in benefits but only \)900 in qualified expenses. This correction adds no dataset imputation, reporting-status input or missing-information validation. Carryovers, forfeitures and self-employment plan deductions remain outside this employer-benefit calculation.

Supply this input separately from cdcc_qualified_care_expenses, which remains the computed expense helper for the later credit; overriding that helper does not establish the employer-assistance expense amount. The model retains its qualifying-person checks and subtracts excluded benefits once from creditable costs. The model keeps the existing employer-benefit payroll input convention. Keeping the assistance expense input independent also avoids a cycle through modeled childcare subsidies and adjusted gross income.

California applies its own earnings and dollar limits directly to the independently supplied qualified amount, as FTB 3506 Part IV requires. Higher California earnings, including its different HSA treatment, cannot override the qualified-expense ceiling. The model adds the positive federal-minus-California exclusion difference back to California adjusted gross income. When California’s exclusion exceeds the federal exclusion, the current addback returns zero. The model does not calculate a California subtraction for that difference. This correction leaves that adjustment route unverified and does not establish complete historical validation of California taxes.

Saver’s Credit examples check the published inclusive AGI ceilings and fractional income above them. Generated populations repeat these checks for all five filing statuses in every year from 2017 through 2026.

The 60 wage cases do not exercise every flagged provision. They do not establish complete coverage of itemized deductions, AMT preferences, capital gains, retirement transactions, identification compliance or special filing situations. The TIN input does not encode issuance dates or every identification requirement; aggregate childcare spending cannot distinguish each child’s eligible expense share in mixed-age families. Fractional age inputs can express the former IRA age boundary, but integer survey ages do not reconstruct birthdays.

This validation covers selected federal household calculations. It does not establish historical accuracy for state taxes, benefit programs, household net resources or national 2017 population estimates. Those uses require their own policy-date, input and population checks.
