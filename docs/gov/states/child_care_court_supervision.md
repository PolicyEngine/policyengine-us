# Court supervision and child-care subsidies

Policy review: September 25, 2026. Scope: the child-care subsidy programs modeled by PolicyEngine US, across the 50 states and DC.

[45 CFR 98.20(a)(1)(ii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-98/subpart-C/section-98.20) makes the older-child court-supervision route a **state option**. The correct national implementation therefore includes negative cases. Court supervision, a court order directing child care, and a protective-services agency referral or approval are separate facts in law. Court status never establishes disability.

`is_under_court_supervision` is the only input: an annual person boolean, default false. Where a state's court-related rule requires a court order or case plan with specific content (directing child care, constant supervision or constant care), that content is treated as a verification detail and the rule is modeled through court supervision, as `is_disabled` stands in for verified incapacity. Court status does not establish an agency referral, authorization or waiver; a financial waiver or rate applies only where the state rule attaches it to the court category itself. New formulas are limited to child care.

## Routes not modeled

- Texas: the court-related route is DFPS-authorized and funded protective child care (40 TAC 809.49), an agency authorization. The formula is unchanged, and tests confirm court supervision alone has no effect.
- Delaware and New Jersey: DFS and CP&P protective-care referrals, and their payment and copay treatment, are agency routes separate from court supervision.
- South Carolina: activity and copay waivers require separate DSS approval.

These changes retain existing disability proxies, annual-age approximations, and enrollment/renewal limitations unless expressly addressed above. Generic protective-services inputs also retain their existing meaning; court supervision must not be inferred from them. This is not an assertion that every protective-services pathway or every separate TANF/foster-care program is fully modeled.

## Maryland uses the approved plan

The [approved FFY2025–27 plan, §2.2.1(c)](https://earlychildhood.marylandpublicschools.org/system/files/filedepot/12/acf-118_ccdf_ffy_2025-2027_plan_approved_updated_6.25.25_amendment_request_1.pdf) elects the court-supervision extension through age 18. The official current [COMAR 13A.14.06.02B(11),(16)](https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/13A.14.06.02) instead defines an older eligible child through disability. The agency's public eligibility guidance also describes the disability route. The plan checkbox was checked visually; the discrepancy is not an extraction ambiguity.

The Maryland implementation follows the FFY2022–24 and FFY2025–27 plans from October 1, 2021; the FFY2019–21 plan elected No. It documents the differing COMAR definition. This source choice does not treat the plan as a regulatory amendment or infer any financial/activity waiver.

## New Jersey and Delaware use the approved plan

The [approved New Jersey FFY2025–27 plan, §2.2.1(c)](https://www.childcarenj.gov/ChildCareNJ/media/media_library/CCDF_State_Plan_for_New_Jersey_FFY25-27.pdf#page=20) elects the court-supervision extension through age 18. [N.J.A.C. 10:15-1.3(a)](https://www.nj.gov/humanservices/notices/documents/rules-and-regulations/NJAC%2010_15%20CHILD%20CARE%20SERVICES.PDF#page=21) instead lists only the under-13, under-19 disabled and cash-assistance-linked child definitions. The [approved Delaware FFY2025–27 plan, §2.2.1(c)](https://mychildde.org/wp-content/uploads/4.15.26-ACF-118-CCDF-FFY-2025-2027-For-Delaware.pdf#page=21) (approved November 9, 2024) makes the same election; the DSSM handbook describes only the under-13, under-19 disabled and DFS-referral routes.

Both implementations follow the approved plan from October 1, 2024, the current plan period start, through the `court_supervision_extension` parameter, consistent with the Nevada treatment. The court route uses the existing under-19 special-needs ceiling and keeps the ordinary income, activity, immigration and copay rules; it does not create a CP&P or DFS referral. The earlier calculation is retained before October 2024 because the FFY2022–24 elections were not verified; that modeling boundary does not assert that either state first elected the option in 2024.
