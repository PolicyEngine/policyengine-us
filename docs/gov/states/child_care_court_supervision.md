# Court supervision and child-care subsidies

Policy review: September 20, 2026. Scope: the child-care subsidy programs modeled by PolicyEngine US, across the 50 states and DC. The implementations are split across related changes; this inventory does not imply every change is already on the default branch.

[45 CFR 98.20(a)(1)(ii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-98/subpart-C/section-98.20) makes the older-child court-supervision route a **state option**. The correct national implementation therefore includes negative cases. Court supervision, a care-specific court order, and a protective-services agency referral are separate facts. None automatically establishes disability or every financial waiver.

`is_under_court_supervision` and `requires_childcare_under_court_order` are annual person inputs, default false. State-specific inputs record additional facts that cannot safely be inferred from general court status. New formulas are limited to child care.

## Conditional pathways and inputs

- California: `ca_calworks_child_care_has_qualifying_court_supervision` records the specified juvenile-code basis; `ca_calworks_child_care_meets_age_18_school_requirement` records the full MPP school condition. Stage One is the modeled program. Separate extended nonminor-foster pathways are not newly modeled here.
- Colorado: `co_ccap_has_verified_additional_care_needs` records the required documented care need, without setting disability.
- Delaware and New Jersey: `de_poc_has_dfs_referral` and `nj_ccap_has_cpp_referral` record actual agency child-care referrals. `nj_ccap_cpp_copay_waived` records a case-specific waiver; a partially reduced assessed amount can be supplied through `nj_ccap_copay`.
- Hawaii: `hi_ccap_court_ordered_protective_care` combines the existing protective-services input, a care-specific court order, and the under-18 child definition. The income exemption and the activity/age conditions are distinct.
- Iowa: `ia_cca_court_ordered_care_approved` records HHS approval. A court order alone does not authorize CCA payment for an over-age child. The [agency manual, pp42–43](https://hhs.iowa.gov/media/3816#page=47) also retains citizenship/alienage requirements.
- South Carolina: `sc_ccap_court_care_activity_waived` and `sc_ccap_court_care_copay_waived` independently record DSS-approved family waivers and require an eligible court-supervised child.
- Texas: `tx_ccs_dfps_authorized` records authorization and funding by DFPS; `tx_ccs_dfps_assessed_parent_share` records any assessed parent share. Existing schedule inputs are preserved.

Child-specific referral payment paths in DE, IA, NJ and TX use each child's reported `pre_subsidy_childcare_expenses`. Supply those expenses explicitly for older children and mixed referral households: the shared default allocation of aggregate household expenses uses the general child population and cannot identify which child's care an agency approved. Agency-approved provider eligibility, hours/units and case decisions must still be supplied through existing program inputs where they cannot be derived.

These changes retain existing disability proxies, annual-age approximations, and enrollment/renewal limitations unless expressly addressed above. Generic protective-services inputs also retain their existing meaning; court supervision must not be inferred from them. This is not an assertion that every protective-services pathway or every separate TANF/foster-care program is fully modeled.

## Maryland requires authoritative reconciliation

The [approved FFY2025–27 plan, §2.2.1(c)](https://earlychildhood.marylandpublicschools.org/system/files/filedepot/12/acf-118_ccdf_ffy_2025-2027_plan_approved_updated_6.25.25_amendment_request_1.pdf) elects the court-supervision extension through age 18. The official current [COMAR 13A.14.06.02B(11),(16)](https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/13A.14.06.02) instead defines an older eligible child through disability. The agency's public eligibility guidance also describes the disability route. The plan checkbox was checked visually; the discrepancy is not an extraction ambiguity.

No rule amendment or agency directive reconciling those provisions was identified. The Maryland formula is therefore unchanged. An MSDE clarification should identify the operative authority, qualifying court/case conditions, upper birthday boundary and effective date before encoding this route. The nationwide implementation remains incomplete while this conflict is unresolved.
