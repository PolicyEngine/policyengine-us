VIDOL ANNOUNCES 2026 UNEMPLOYMENT INSURANCE TAX RATES

Virgin Islands Department of Labor (VIDOL) Commissioner Gary Molloy, Ph.D. announces that VIDOL has finalized the 2026 Unemployment Insurance (UI) tax rates for employers, effective January 1, 2026. These rates are calculated in accordance with Title 24 Chapter 12 Section 308 of the Virgin Islands Code and reflect VIDOL’s commitment to maintaining a balanced and fair system for employers and employees.

The 2026 rates reflect VIDOL’s continued commitment to maintaining a fair, balanced, and financially sound Unemployment Insurance system that supports both employers and workers.

Key 2026 Tax Rate Information

• Average Contribution Rate: 4.04% (a decrease from 4.06% in 2025 and 4.11% in 2024).
• Individual Employer Rates: Rates vary based on each employer’s experience history rating and are assigned interval groups, ranging from 2.43% to 5.4% .

How the Rates Are Determined

UI tax rates are reviewed and adjusted annually to ensure the long-term stability of the Unemployment Insurance Trust Fund. For 2026, VIDOL’s analysis confirms that the payroll variation method , which considers total payroll growth and trust fund performance, is working as intended to stabilize rates and prevent unnecessary increases.

The Taxable Wage Base for 2026 has been set at $32,100 , equal to 60 percent of the Average Annual Wage in insured employment. This adjustment reflects overall wage growth while ensuring that employer contributions remain aligned with the financial needs of the Trust Fund.

Why Rates Decreased in 2026

As employment and payroll levels increase and the Trust Fund strengthens, the system automatically adjusts to reduce employer tax rates. This approach prevents employers from paying more than necessary when the fund is healthy, while still maintaining sufficient reserves to support workers during economic slowdowns.

Commissioner Dr. Molloy emphasized that the 2026 rates demonstrate “responsible stewardship of the Unemployment Insurance Trust Fund, fairness to employers, and continued protection for workers who rely on unemployment benefits during periods of economic transition.”

Looking Ahead to 2026

The Department anticipates further rate decreases in 2027, as the repayment of advances continues to reduce financial pressures on the Unemployment Insurance Trust Fund. Additionally, absent any major economic disruptions, employers can expect continued reductions in the rate structure over time.
