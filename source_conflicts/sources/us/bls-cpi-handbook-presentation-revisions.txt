The CPI, unlike many other statistical series, does not rely on respondents to send data to the BLS national office; CPI data collectors collect almost all of the data for the CPI-U and CPI-W. All data are scheduled to be received in time for the calculation of indexes for the appropriate month, so routine revisions for late-arriving data are not made. The CPI-U and CPI-W are commonly used in escalation agreements and to adjust pensions and tax brackets; consequently, revisions can be costly for the users of these indexes, so these series are final when issued. Additional information on escalation contracts is available in the “How to Use the Consumer Price Index for Escalation” factsheet.
In the rare case when BLS discovers that an error was made while collecting or compiling information, we issue a correction to the affected series in accordance with BLS policies. When errors in current data are found prior to publication, efforts are exerted to correct and certify the data before indexes are released. In cases where error fixes cannot be made before the scheduled release, data values are suppressed from publication until they are verified as correct. When an error is discovered in past published values of an index series, an analysis is conducted to determine if corrected data yields inflation estimates that are statistically different from those published. If the error does not warrant correction, published data are maintained as originally issued.
Seasonal adjustment is a statistical procedure that adjusts for normal seasonal variations to help decisionmakers better understand underlying economic changes (e.g., gas prices that typically rise each summer). Each year, with the release of January data, seasonal adjustment factors are recalculated to reflect price movements from the previous calendar year. This routine recalculation will result in revisions to seasonally adjusted indexes for the previous 5 years. Additional information on seasonal adjustment is available on the seasonal adjustment webpage.
